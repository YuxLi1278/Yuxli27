This paper studies recursive alignment: how one generation's outputs, judgments,
and methods shape the alignment of later systems. It separates two preservation
challenges: keeping supervision reliable as capability grows, and retaining the
behaviors and preferences that supervision is intended to protect.

The survey reviews recursive curation and synthetic-data feedback, pluralistic
preference aggregation, amplification and distillation, debate and other oversight
methods, weak-to-strong transfer, recursive evaluators, and automated alignment
research. Across these approaches, it compares assumptions and failure modes,
highlighting that local gains may not survive optimization and reuse. It concludes
that evaluation should track distributional coverage, stakeholder representation,
and oversight quality across successive training stages.
